#1.1. Connections

!!! definition "Definition 1.1.1"

    A (**Koszul**) **connection** on a $\bbk$-vector bundle $E$ is a $\bbk$-linear map $\nabla\: \Gamma(E) \to \Gamma(T^\vee M \otimes_\bbR E)$ satisfying the Leibniz rule

    \[ \nabla(fs) = df \otimes s + f \,\nabla s\]

    for all $f \in C^\infty(M; \bbk)$ and $s \in \Gamma(E)$.
    We denote the set of all connections on $E$ by $\Conn(E)$.

Given connection $\nabla \in \Conn(E)$, a vector field $X \in \frakX(M)$ and a section $s \in \Gamma(E)$, we denote the image of $s$ under the composition
<script type="text/tikz">
\newcommand{\bbR}{\mathbb{R}}
\newcommand{\ev}{\mathrm{ev}}
\newcommand{\id}{\mathrm{id}}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=normal]
    \Gamma(E)
        \arrow[r, "\nabla"]
    & \Gamma(T^\vee M \otimes_\bbR E)
        \arrow[r, "{\langle {-}, X \rangle_{\ev} \otimes_{\bbR} {\id}}"]
    &[3em] \Gamma(E)
\end{tikzcd}
</script>
by $\nablax{X} s$. Then, $\nablax{X}\: \Gamma(E) \to \Gamma(E)$ becomes a $\bbk$-linear endomorphism. In terms of the map $\nablax{X}$, the Leibniz rule is written as

\[ \nabla_X(fs) = Xf \cdot s + f\,\nablax{X}s \text. \]

!!! example "Example 1.1.1"

    Consider the trivial bundle $\ul{\bbR}{}^m$ of rank $m$ over $M$. A section of $\ul{\bbR}{}^m$ is simply a $\bbR^m$-valued function on $M$. The differential $d\: C^\infty(M; \bbR^m) \to \Omega^1(M;\bbR^m)$ satisfies the Leibniz rule, and hence defines a connection on $\ul{\bbR}{}^m$. This connection is called the **trivial connection**.

!!! proposition "Proposition 1.1.1"

    Any vector bundle admits a connection.

??? proof "Proof"

    Let $E \to M$ be any smooth $\bbk$-vector bundle on a smooth manifold. Let $\{(U_\alpha, \Phi_\alpha)\}_{\alpha \in I}$ be a trivializing open cover for $E$, and let $\{\psi_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in I}$ be a partition of unity subordinate to $\{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in I}$. For each $\alpha \in I$, $E|_{U_\alpha}$ is trivial, so there exists a connection, say $\nabla^\alpha$, on $E|_{U_\alpha}$. Given $X \in \frakX(M)$ and $s \in \Gamma(E)$, let

    \[ \nabla_X s = \sum_{\alpha \in I} \psi_\alpha \nabla^\alpha_{X|_{U_\alpha}} (s|_{U_\alpha}) \text. \]

    Here, each summand is extended by zero outside $U_\alpha$. Since the sum on the right-hand side is locally finite, $\nabla$ is well-defined. It is clear that $\nabla$ is $C^\infty(M; \bbk)$-linear in $X$ and $\bbk$-linear in $s$. If $f \in C^\infty(M; \bbk)$, then

    \[ \begin{aligned} \nabla_X(fs) &= \sum_{\alpha \in I} \psi_\alpha \nabla^\alpha_{X|_{U_\alpha}} ((fs)|_{U_\alpha}) \\ &= \sum_{\alpha \in I} \psi_\alpha X|_{U_\alpha}(f|_{U_\alpha}) s|_{U_\alpha} + \sum_{\alpha \in I} \psi_\alpha f|_{U_\alpha} \nabla^\alpha_{X|_{U_\alpha}} (s|_{U_\alpha}) \\ &= X(f)s + f \nabla_X s \text. \end{aligned} \]

    Hence, $\nabla$ is a connection on $E$.

Suppose that there is a pairing $\mu\: E \otimes_\bbk E' \to E''$ that is a morphism of $\bbk$-vector bundles over $M$. Then, $\mu$ together with the exterior product ${\wedge}\: \Lambda^*(T^\vee M) \otimes_\bbR \Lambda^*(T^\vee M) \to \Lambda^*(T^\vee M)$ defines a morphism

<script type="text/tikz">
\providecommand{\bbk}{\Bbbk}
\providecommand{\bbR}{\mathbb{R}}
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=tiny]
    (\Lambda^*(T^\vee M) \otimes_\bbR E) \otimes_\bbk (\Lambda^*(T^\vee M) \otimes_\bbR E') \arrow[d, phantom, "\cong" description, sloped] \\
    (\Lambda^*(T^\vee M) \otimes_\bbR \Lambda^*(T^\vee M)) \otimes_\bbR (E \otimes_\bbk E') \arrow[r, "{\wedge} \otimes_\bbR \mu"] & \Lambda^*(T^\vee M) \otimes_\bbR E'' \text,
\end{tikzcd}
</script>

and thus a $C^\infty(M; \bbk)$-bilinear map

\[ \Omega^*(M; E) \times \Omega^*(M; E') \to \Omega^*(M; E'') \text. \]

By abuse of notation, we denote this $C^\infty(M; \bbk)$-bilinear map by $\wedge$.

!!! example "Example"

    The evaluation pairing ${\ev}\: E^\vee \otimes_\bbk E \to \underline{\smash[b]{\bbk}}$ defines a wedge product

    \[ {\wedge}\: \Omega^*(M; E^\vee) \times \Omega^*(M; E) \to \Omega^*(M) \text. \]

    Similarly, the pairing ${\ev}\: \End_\bbk(E) \otimes_\bbk E \to E$, obtained by tensoring the evaluation pairing above with $E$, defines a wedge product

    \[ {\wedge}\: \Omega^*(M; \End_\bbk(E)) \times \Omega^*(M; E) \to \Omega^*(M; E) \text. \]

Let $\nabla, \nabla' \in \Conn(E)$ be connections on $E$. Then, we have

\[ (\nabla - \nabla')(fs) = (df \otimes s + f\,\nabla s) - (df \otimes s + f \,\nabla' s) = f(\nabla - \nabla')s \]

for any $f \in C^\infty(M; \bbk)$ and $s \in \Gamma(E)$. It follows that $D(\nabla, \nabla') = \nabla - \nabla'$ becomes a section of $E^\vee \otimes_\bbk (T^\vee M \otimes_\bbR E) \cong T^\vee M \otimes_\bbR \End_\bbk(E)$. This section

\[ D(\nabla, \nabla') = \nabla - \nabla' \in \Gamma(T^\vee M \otimes_\bbR \End_\bbk(E)) \]

is often called the **difference tensor** between $\nabla$ and $\nabla'$.

!!! proposition "Proposition 1.1.2"

    The set $\Conn(E)$ is an affine space over the vector space $\Gamma(T^\vee M \otimes_\bbR \End_\bbk(E))$.

??? proof "Proof"

    If $\alpha \in \Gamma(T^\vee M \otimes_\bbR \End_\bbk(E))$, then any section $s \in \Gamma(E)$ can be evaluated in the $\End_\bbk(E)$-component of $\alpha$. In this way, we obtain a section $\alpha(s) \in \Gamma(T^\vee M \otimes_\bbR E)$. Fix a connection $\nabla \in \Conn(E)$. Then,

    \[ (\nabla + \alpha)(fs) = df \otimes s + f \,\nabla s + f \alpha(s) = df \otimes s + f(\nabla + \alpha)s \text, \]

    so $\nabla + \alpha$ becomes a connection on $E$.
    We claim that the map $\Gamma(T^\vee M \otimes_\bbR \End_\bbk(E)) \to \Conn(E)$ defined by $\alpha \mapsto \nabla + \alpha$ is a bijection. This map is clearly injective, and it is surjective since the difference between two connections is always an element of $\Gamma(T^\vee M \otimes_\bbR \End_\bbk(E))$.

### Connections on the trivial bundle

Suppose that we are given a connection $\nabla \in \Conn(E)$.
Fix local coordinates $(U, \phi)$ on $M$ and a local frame $\{e_j\}_{j = 1}^m$ of $E$ on $U$. Then, we can find smooth functions $\Gamma_{ij}^k \in C^\infty(U; \bbk)$ such that

\[ \nablax{\partial_{x_i}} e_j = \sum_{k = 1}^m \Gamma_{ij}^k e_k \text. \]

The functions $\Gamma_{ij}^k$ are called the **connection coefficients** of $\nabla$ with respect to $\phi$ and $e$.
A straightforward computation shows that, for a local vector field $X = \sum_{i = 1}^n X_i \partial_{x_i}$ and a local section $s = \sum_{j = 1}^m s_j e_j$, one has

\[ \nablax{X} s = \sum_{k = 1}^m \left( X(s_k) + \sum_{i = 1}^n \sum_{j = 1}^m \Gamma_{ij}^k X_i s_j \right) e_k \text. \]

Conversely, any connection on the trivial bundle arises in this way.
